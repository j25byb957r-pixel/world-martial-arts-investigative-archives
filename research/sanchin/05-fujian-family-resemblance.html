Fujian Family Resemblance

The archive uses “family resemblance” deliberately. It allows us to recognize recurring regional features without pretending that resemblance solves the genealogy.

Regional relationshipWhat can be comparedWhat resemblance cannot proveWhy the concept is useful

A probable regional relationship

Historical research on Uechi Sanchin strongly supports looking toward Fujian, yet surviving Fujian forms do not provide one obvious, exact match for the Uechi version. That is not a failure of the investigation. It may reflect transmission, adaptation, branching lineages, lost material, or the simple fact that related traditions change over time.

Archive classification: comparative regional relationship. Stronger than coincidence, weaker than a documented teacher-to-student genealogy.

What can be compared responsibly

Useful comparison includes foundational-form status, compact stance work, stepping, breathing coordination, push/draw relationships, advance and retreat, hard/soft organization, and repeated structural training. No single feature carries the argument by itself; the value comes from examining clusters of features alongside chronology and geography.

What resemblance cannot prove

A similar movement, name, or training idea cannot identify a specific ancestor school. Convergence is also possible: different traditions may solve similar training problems in similar ways. That is why every resemblance must be paired with a historical question: who, where, when, and through what surviving evidence?

Why the family-resemblance model helps

This model gives the archive room to study evolution. A practice may preserve an older principle while changing outward form. Conversely, two systems may look similar today without sharing a direct transmission. Both possibilities stay open until evidence narrows them.
