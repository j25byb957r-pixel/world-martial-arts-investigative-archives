Sanchin dossier • 02

San Zhan / Sam Chien / Sanchin

The names are close enough to demand comparison, but language alone cannot establish descent. This page explains what the terminology contributes to the investigation and where its evidentiary value stops.

The namesThree battlesComparative valueLimits

The names across traditions

Southern Chinese traditions use forms of the name San Zhan or Sam Chien, while Okinawan karate uses Sanchin. The resemblance reflects related readings of Chinese characters commonly translated as “three battles” or “three conflicts.” That linguistic relationship is significant because it places the terms within a shared East Asian vocabulary rather than treating them as unrelated inventions.

Archive classification: comparative linguistic evidence. Similar names strengthen the case for historical investigation, but do not identify a specific teacher, date, or transmission route.

What “three battles” can and cannot tell us

Different schools have interpreted the “three” in different ways—body, mind, and breath; internal and external struggles; or technical phases. The archive records those interpretations as lineage-specific or practitioner explanations unless a historical source can establish that one interpretation was original.

This is especially important because a memorable translation can easily become mistaken for an ancient universal doctrine.

Why the terminology still matters

When a related name appears alongside other parallels—foundational status, compact stepping, breath coordination, repeated structural training, and forward/backward movement—the terminology becomes one part of a broader comparative pattern. The more independent features align, the stronger the case for studying a regional relationship.

Where the evidence stops

A shared name does not prove that one surviving White Crane form was copied directly into Uechi-Ryū. Nor does it prove that all forms called San Zhan or Sanchin once had identical choreography. The archive therefore treats terminology as a clue that opens the door, not as the final answer.
