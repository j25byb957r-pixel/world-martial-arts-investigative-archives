Sanchin dossier • 04

Forward and Backward Study

Direction is not a minor detail in this investigation. Wakayama-era accounts describe beginners working Sanchin forward and backward, giving the archive a historical reason to examine directional continuity across Uechi and Fujian traditions.

Wakayama evidenceWhy direction mattersThe 3+3 questionStructural interpretation

Wakayama evidence

Retrospective Uechi historical accounts describe new students spending an extended introductory period on Sanchin movement, including forward and backward travel. This establishes bidirectional practice as part of the remembered early teaching environment rather than a purely modern reconstruction.

Archive classification: historical account for forward/backward Sanchin practice. The exact number of steps and exact choreography require separate evidence.

Why direction matters

A form that can be trained while advancing and retreating may preserve a different training logic than one understood only as a fixed performance sequence. Directional study can therefore reveal what a lineage emphasized about continuity, balance, orientation, and organization under change.

The exact three-forward / three-back question

Fujian White Crane material gives us comparative evidence for a three-step advance/retreat pattern. The existence of that pattern in a living Fujian transmission is significant, but the archive still separates it from the question of whether Kanbun Uechi practiced the identical numerical sequence.

That distinction is why the dedicated Three Forward / Three Back dossier remains a separate research branch.

Structural interpretation

The project's working idea of maintaining organized frontal relationship while direction changes is useful for comparison. It is recorded as interpretation unless a historical source explicitly describes the same principle in similar language.
