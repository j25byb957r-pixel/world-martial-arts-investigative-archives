American lineage dossier

American Uechi-Ryū: Michigan lineage and connected pioneers

A research file recognizing practitioners who preserved, taught, documented, and developed Uechi-Ryū in the United States, while distinguishing documentary history from practitioner testimony and later comparative interpretation.

LineageDocumented / corroborated

Alan / Allen J. Horton

Uechi community histories identify Horton as an early Michigan practitioner who studied under Ryuko Tomoyose and began teaching Uechi-Ryū in Michigan during the 1960s. Historical accounts identify William L. Keith as his student and teaching partner.

LineageDocumented / corroborated

William L. Keith

Michigan lineage accounts identify Keith as a student and teaching partner of Horton. Donald Joyner's biography records beginning Uechi-Ryū training in 1972 at the Kalamazoo YMCA under Steve Fuller and Bill Keith.

American pioneerStrongly documented

James Thompson

Thompson began training at the Futenma headquarters dojo in Okinawa in 1962 and later became a major teacher in the Michigan line. Regional histories connect him with Kanei Uechi and Ryuko Tomoyose and identify him as Donald Joyner's formal instructor.

Regional recognitionStrongly documented

Donald B. Joyner

Joyner connects the Kalamazoo Uechi community with Grand Rapids. His record documents training under Steve Fuller, Bill Keith, and James Thompson and the later operation of Joyner's Professional Karate School, a predecessor in the regional East West lineage.

American contextDocumented

George E. Mattson

Mattson is retained as a central pioneer and documentarian of American Uechi-Ryū and an important link in the wider network surrounding the Michigan practitioners.

American pioneerFirst-party record

Alan Dollar

Alan Dollar began Uechi-Ryū training in Okinawa in 1974 under Seiyu Shinjo and Kiyohide Shinjo, received his Shodan at Futenma in 1975, and later received instructor certification from Kanei Uechi. His surviving biography documents extensive subsequent Okinawan training and his establishment of a public Uechi-Ryū dojo in the San Francisco Bay Area.

Dollar's record also intersects directly with other pioneers in this archive: he trained at George Mattson's dojo, attended Michigan camps conducted by James Thompson, hosted Thompson and Mattson among visiting instructors, and in 1992 was promoted to Rokudan by Shinyu Gushi, James Thompson, and Kiyohide Shinjo.

East West Karate Center

Regional histories preserve a Grand Rapids continuity from William Keith and James Thompson through Donald Joyner and into the later East West Karate Center. Differences in surviving naming and directory timelines remain openly marked for further investigation.

Practitioner testimony

James Wagar's direct connection

Archive contributor James Wagar reports personally training and working with several of the practitioners represented in this American Uechi-Ryū network, including Alan Dollar and members of the Michigan lineage. This statement is preserved as first-person practitioner testimony. It is not substituted for independent documentary evidence; where photographs, certificates, seminar records, correspondence, video, dojo records, or contemporaneous publications become available, they can be attached to this testimony as corroborating evidence.

Preservation purpose

History first; comparison second

The documented American transmission provides an evidentiary foundation for the archive's broader comparison of Okinawan and Chinese material. The Heaven-and-Earth inquiry remains separately labeled: relationships such as upper/lower, rising/sinking, expansion/contraction, hard/soft, and advance/retreat may be investigated without attributing a single doctrine to these practitioners unless direct evidence establishes it.
