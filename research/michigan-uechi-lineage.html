Michigan Uechi-Ryū: Horton, Keith, Thompson, Joyner, Mattson, and East West Karate

A research file recognizing early American and Michigan practitioners who preserved, taught, documented, and developed Uechi-Ryū in the United States. It also provides a historical foundation for the archive’s comparative study of structure, complementary forces, and the Heaven-and-Earth concept while keeping philosophical interpretation distinct from documented lineage history.

LineageDocumented / corroborated

Alan / Allen J. Horton

Uechi community histories identify Horton as an early Michigan practitioner who studied under Ryuko Tomoyose and began teaching Uechi-Ryū in Michigan during the 1960s. A 2004 historical discussion identifies him as the first Uechi-Ryū practitioner in Michigan and William L. Keith as his student and teaching partner.

William L. Keith

Michigan lineage accounts identify William Keith as a student and teaching partner of Horton. Keith is directly connected to Donald Joyner’s early training: Joyner’s biography records beginning in 1972 at the Kalamazoo YMCA under Steve Fuller and Bill Keith, with Keith later recommending Joyner for his first-degree black-belt promotion.

James Thompson

James Thompson deserves distinct recognition in the American history of Uechi-Ryū. Uechi-Con records that Thompson began training at the headquarters dojo in Futenma, Okinawa, in 1962 and spent many years training in Okinawa with Uechi-Ryū masters. He later founded and led the Kokusai Kyokai branch of Uechi-Ryū Karate-do.

Michigan lineage history states that William Keith’s Kalamazoo dojo passed to Thompson, who had studied with Kanei Uechi and Ryuko Tomoyose. Thompson became a major teacher in the Michigan line: Don Joyner identifies Thompson as his formal instructor, and Thompson repeatedly served as the senior instructor responsible for Joyner’s rank examinations and advancement. George Mattson’s historical list also places James Thompson among the early registered American Uechi-Ryū black belts.

Donald B. Joyner

Don Joyner is an important bridge between the Kalamazoo Uechi community and Grand Rapids. His biography records his start in 1972 under Steve Fuller and Bill Keith, followed by formal training under James Thompson. Joyner operated Joyner’s Professional Karate School in Grand Rapids from 1991 to 2002, training students who continued the regional lineage. Fourth Form’s local history identifies Joyner’s school as the predecessor to the later East West Karate Center.

Joyner’s record also documents continued engagement with the wider Uechi-Ryū community through seminars, camps, rank examinations, and training trips. George Mattson’s Uechi-Ryu site presented Joyner as a WinterFest instructor, providing another independent connection between Joyner and the broader American Uechi network.

George E. Mattson

George Mattson belongs in this dossier as a major figure in the development and documentation of Uechi-Ryū in the United States. He should not be inserted automatically as Horton’s direct teacher: Horton’s documented connection to Ryuko Tomoyose stands on its own. Mattson instead provides essential national context and preserved historical records for the American Uechi network.

East West Karate Center

Fourth Form Martial Arts preserves a Grand Rapids lineage from William Keith and James Thompson through Donald Joyner, stating that Joyner opened Joyner’s Professional Karate in 1991 and that Jon Olson later renamed the dojo East West Karate Center in 2003. Contemporary directory material also associates East West with William Keith. Rather than erase the difference, the archive retains it as a timeline question involving instruction, ownership, naming, or directory lag.

Working transmission map

Regional chain and wider American context

Ryuko Tomoyose → Alan/Allen J. Horton → William L. Keith → Michigan students. James Thompson forms a major Okinawa-to-Michigan branch through his training in Futenma and with Uechi-Ryū masters including Kanei Uechi and Ryuko Tomoyose. In Michigan, Keith’s dojo passed to Thompson according to Fourth Form’s history. A documented Grand Rapids continuation then runs through William Keith / James Thompson → Donald B. Joyner → Joyner’s Professional Karate → later East West Karate lineage. George E. Mattson is retained alongside these lines as a central pioneer and documentarian of American Uechi-Ryū.

Preservation purpose

History first; comparison second

This archive recognizes these practitioners because their teaching, organizations, records, and students helped preserve Uechi-Ryū in the United States. Their historical record gives later researchers something concrete against which older Chinese and Okinawan material can be compared.

The archive’s study of the Heaven-and-Earth concept is therefore maintained as a separate comparative and interpretive inquiry. The project may examine complementary relationships such as upper/lower, rising/sinking, expansion/contraction, hard/soft, advance/retreat, and the organization of the body between ground and sky. These comparisons are not presented as proof that the named American pioneers themselves taught a single doctrine called “Heaven and Earth,” unless direct evidence establishes that claim. The purpose is to preserve historical truth while allowing the deeper structural and philosophical correlations to be investigated openly.
