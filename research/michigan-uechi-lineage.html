Michigan Uechi-Ryū: Horton, Keith, Joyner, Mattson, and East West Karate

A research file documenting the Michigan transmission associated with Alan/Allen J. Horton, William L. Keith, Donald B. Joyner, George E. Mattson, Kalamazoo, Grand Rapids, and East West Karate Center. Sources are compared rather than forced into a single narrative when details differ.

LineageDocumented / corroborated

Alan / Allen J. Horton

Multiple Uechi community sources identify Horton as an early Michigan practitioner who trained under Ryuko Tomoyose and began teaching in Michigan in the 1960s. Spelling varies between Alan, Allen, and Allan across surviving sources.

LineageDocumented / corroborated

William L. Keith

Michigan lineage accounts identify William Keith as a student and teaching partner of Horton. Keith is also directly connected to Donald Joyner’s early training: Joyner’s published biography states that he began Uechi-Ryū training in 1972 at the Kalamazoo YMCA under Steve Fuller and Bill Keith, and that Keith later recommended Joyner for his 1977 Shodan promotion.

Donald B. Joyner

Don Joyner is an important bridge between the Kalamazoo Uechi community and Grand Rapids. His biography records his start in 1972 under Steve Fuller and Bill Keith, followed by formal training under James Thompson. Joyner operated Joyner’s Professional Karate School in Grand Rapids from 1991 to 2002, training students who continued the regional lineage. Fourth Form’s local history identifies Joyner’s school as the predecessor to the later East West Karate Center.

Joyner’s record also documents continued engagement with the wider Uechi-Ryū community through seminars, camps, rank examinations, and training trips. George Mattson’s Uechi-Ryu site presented Joyner as a WinterFest instructor, providing another independent connection between Joyner and the broader American Uechi network.

George E. Mattson

George Mattson belongs in this dossier as a major figure in the development and documentation of Uechi-Ryū in the United States. He should not be inserted automatically as Horton’s direct teacher: Horton’s documented connection to Ryuko Tomoyose stands on its own. Mattson instead provides essential national context for the American network in which Michigan Uechi developed.

Dojo historyDocumented with timeline questions

East West Karate Center

Fourth Form Martial Arts preserves a Grand Rapids lineage from William Keith and James Thompson through Donald Joyner, stating that Joyner opened Joyner’s Professional Karate in 1991 and that Jon Olson later renamed the dojo East West Karate Center in 2003. Contemporary directory material also associates East West with William Keith. Rather than erase the difference, the archive retains it as a timeline question involving instruction, ownership, naming, or directory lag.
