Perspectives • Ideas • Open inquiry

A place for ideas without disguising them as facts.

Some observations deserve preservation before the historical record can confirm or reject them. This section keeps practitioner interpretation, philosophical connections, comparative possibilities, alternative explanations and developing questions visible while protecting the boundary between opinion and evidence.

Reading rule: an idea may be insightful without being historically established. Every perspective should identify its author or source, its category, the evidence that relates to it, and what remains unresolved.

The goal is not to make every idea equal. It is to preserve useful thinking while making its evidential status unmistakable.

Perspective record format

Perspective — named author or source

Category: practitioner observation, interpretation, philosophical connection, comparative hypothesis, alternative explanation, or open question.

Statement: the idea in the clearest possible form.

Related evidence: documents, chronology, testimony, forms, places or audiovisual examples that bear on the question.

Counterpoint: plausible alternative readings or conflicting evidence.

Status: supported, partially supported, comparative, unresolved, or contradicted.

Why preserve this separately?

Separating perspective from historical assertion lets the archive remain imaginative without becoming careless. Readers can encounter a powerful interpretation, understand who proposed it, inspect what supports it, and decide what further evidence is needed.

This also allows interpretations to evolve without rewriting the documentary record.
