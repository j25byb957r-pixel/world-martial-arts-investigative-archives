Topic key • process

Transmission

A cross-archive portal for tracking how material moves from person to person, place to place, and generation to generation—and how it changes along the way.

Archive standard: chronology is not genealogy. Similarity can suggest a transmission question, but descent requires an identifiable bridge supported by people, places, dates, records, or mutually reinforcing testimony.

People

Teacher, student, family, school, and organizational relationships are recorded separately according to evidence strength.

Places

Fujian, Wakayama, Okinawa, and later international centers are treated as distinct transmission stages rather than collapsed into one story.

Change

The archive tracks continuity, modification, omission, standardization, convergence, and reinterpretation instead of assuming perfect preservation.

Visual record

Videos can document a modern presentation of a lineage, but they do not independently prove earlier ancestry.

Transmission pathway under study

Fujian → Kanbun Uechi → Wakayama → Okinawa → later international lineages. Each step is treated as its own evidentiary problem. The archive does not assume that every feature visible at the end of the chain existed unchanged at the beginning.

Questions the pathway must answer

Which material can be securely associated with Kanbun? Which changes appear during Wakayama and Okinawan development? Which later schools preserve older features, and which reflect later teaching choices? Where does documentary evidence end and lineage memory begin?
