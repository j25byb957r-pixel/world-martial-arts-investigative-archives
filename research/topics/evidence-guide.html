Archive method • evidence guide

How the research keys grade evidence

The archive is designed so that readers can explore freely without losing track of what is documented, what is inherited tradition, what is a living demonstration, and what remains an open historical question.

Documentary evidence

Dated records, identifiable publications, manuscripts, archival material, institutional records, and other sources whose provenance can be checked. These carry the strongest weight for historical claims.

Corroborated historical evidence

Later accounts or multiple independent sources that support the same event, person, period, or relationship even when the original record is incomplete.

Lineage tradition

Information preserved within a school or transmission. It is historically important and should be recorded accurately, but it is distinguished from independently verified documentation.

Practitioner testimony

First-hand recollection, teaching memory, or personal experience. Valuable for preserving living history, especially when dated and attributable, but not automatically treated as documentary proof.

Comparative observation

Visible or conceptual similarities and differences among forms, terminology, structure, direction, and training methods. Comparison creates questions; it does not by itself prove descent.

Open inquiry

A claim or relationship worth investigating for which the archive does not yet have sufficient evidence. Open questions remain visible rather than being filled with assumptions.

Video standard: audiovisual material can document an identified person's or lineage's presentation at the time of recording. It can illustrate similarity, difference, testimony, or living transmission. It cannot by itself establish an older genealogy.

Why this matters

A research archive becomes more useful when uncertainty is preserved rather than hidden. A reader should always be able to tell whether a statement is a historical record, a traditional attribution, a modern comparison, or an interpretation developed by the archive.
