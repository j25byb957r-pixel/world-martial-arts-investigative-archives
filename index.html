Archive principles

  • Preserve context. A technique is not separated from its time, culture, or teaching purpose.
  • Label certainty. Source, testimony, comparison, and hypothesis remain visibly distinct.
  • Compare carefully. Similar movement does not automatically prove shared origin.
  • Keep inquiry open. New evidence can revise an entry without erasing earlier research history.

Investigative method

Evidence before conclusion

Each archive entry is designed to preserve the difference between what a source explicitly says, what practitioners report, what can be compared through movement or terminology, and what remains a hypothesis.

  1. 01
    Receive it

    Record the claim, form, text, oral history, or observation as accurately as possible.

  2. 02
    Examine it

    Locate dates, lineages, translations, independent sources, and conflicting accounts.

  3. 03
    Experience it

    Where appropriate, analyze embodied mechanics without treating experience as historical proof.

  4. 04
    Question it

    Identify assumptions, gaps, alternative explanations, and what evidence would change the conclusion.

  5. 05
    Cultivate understanding

    Publish the current interpretation with its uncertainty visible and revise it when stronger evidence appears.

About the project

A living investigative archive

This project is intended to organize martial arts research into a form that is useful to practitioners, historians, and curious readers. It does not present every tradition as identical and does not treat speculation as established history.

The public repository provides version history, contribution guidance, and transparent changes so that the archive itself has a research trail.
